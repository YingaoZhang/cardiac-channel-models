# hERG Medicinal Chemistry Notes

hERG liability is often associated with combinations of lipophilicity, protonated basic amines, and hydrophobic aromatic regions. Conservative optimization usually tries to reduce excessive LogP, lower pKa or basicity, reduce hydrophobic aromatic surface area, and disrupt strong aromatic stacking while preserving the core pharmacophore.

Typical design moves include replacing tertiary amines with less basic heterocycles or amides when compatible with activity, adding polarity in solvent-exposed regions, shortening hydrophobic linkers, reducing aromatic ring count, and replacing high-risk lipophilic aryl substituents with less hydrophobic alternatives.

RAG evidence should be used as supporting context. Similar non-blockers can suggest tolerated polarity or scaffold patterns, while similar blockers can highlight fragments that deserve attention. Retrieved examples do not prove causality and should be combined with model predictions and medicinal chemistry judgement.
